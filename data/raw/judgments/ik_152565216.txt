payment of rent Rs.85,000/- pm, hence, he adjusted the arrears

amount in the deposit of Rs.3,75,000/- which was paid by the

complainant.    During further cross examination he duly admits

execution of Ex.P.8 and Ex.P9 including the contents which are

the material documents to show jural relationship which was in

is observed that while vacating the petition premises, accused

also acknowledged Ex.P.10 and admits only Kannada version

14. As per the oral evidence, the disputed cheque was

issued by the accused at the time of execution of Ex.P.10 and on

request by the complainant. But as per the contents of Ex.P.12,

those cheques have been obtained by the complainant forcibly.

Further it is observed that after deduction of rent arrears and

other charges, the accused made payment of R.1,90,000/- to the

complainant through cheque bearing No.379530. This payment

was only with respect to the 1st floor premises.   The document

Ex.P.9 was duly admitted by the accused and as per its contents,

lesseee / complainant   had paid / deposited Rs.2,00,000/-. But

in his oral evidence, the accused denied receipt of above said

deposit amount of Rs.2,00,000/-.    Furthermore, it is observed

that as per defense, if the complainant had not deposited amount

of Rs.2,00,000/-, then the accused could have taken legal steps

against the   complainant.    But in absence of any such legal

action, the contents of the documents Ex.P.9 and Ex.P.10

prevails over the oral version of the accused. Hence overall it is

observed that in the oral and documentary evidence sufficient

discrepancy occurred and no corroborative evidence placed by
11
C.C.No.33015 /2023
the accused in order to rebut the initial presumption or to falsify

the contents of the complaint.
15. Further, by observing the demeanor and conduct of

the accused it goes to show that he           cleverly admits the

convenient facts and rest of the facts are denied which is written

in English.   But on admission of his signature, any one can

presume that no person willingly puts signature on blank paper

and in the present case also there is no allegation from the

accused that they have taken his signature on the blank paper.

Hence in absence of any such steps and circumstances, it is

legally presumed that the accused got executed Ex.P.10 by

admitting the payment of Rs.2         lakhs with respect to ground

floor rental premises. Therefore, on perusal of entire contents of

Ex.P.8 and P.9 along with English version of Ex.P.10 it goes to

show that the accused has received deposit of Rs.2 lakhs with

respect to the ground floor premises. But, there is no relevant

documentary or oral evidence by saying that said amount is

refunded to the complainant. Therefore, the amount mentioned

under the disputed cheque is legally recoverable debt.
16. Further it is observed that as on the date of present

complaint, the possession of entire petition premises ie the

ground and the first floor is duly handed over to the accused and

fresh tenants have already occupied the said premises. Hence,

the amount mentioned under the cheque is refundable amount
12
C.C.No.33015 /2023
since Ex.D.1 and Ex.D2 produced by the accused are none other

than the agreement concerned to Ex.P.8.        Therefore,     the

defense version that the accused issued 2 cheques with respect to

the payment of deposit amount concerned to 1 st floor to the

extent of Rs.1,90,000/- is not considerable because the said

amount is duly encashed through one cheque and another

cheque ie the disputed cheque bounced for want of sufficient

funds. Hence, on complying of necessary ingredients of
Sec.138
of N.I.Act, the complainant approached the court claiming the

cheque amount which is legally considerable debt.
17. For the sake of argument that blank signed cheque is

given to the complainant, it is well settled principle of law that

"even a blank cheque leaf      signed   and handed over by the

accused which is towards some payment, would attract

presumption
u/s.139
of N.I.Act in absence of cogent evidence to

show that cheque was not issued in discharge of debt". Thus no

discrepancy had emerged out of the cross examination which

may demolish complainant version even on the touch stone of

preponderance of probabilities. Thus, accused has not disputed

the loan transaction.    Hence, amount     mentioned under the

cheque is legally recoverable debt.
18. If the amount is not repaid in any other form before

the due date or if there is no other undertaking or agreement

between the parties to defer the payment of amount, the cheque
13
C.C.No.33015 /2023
issued as security would     mature for presentation        and the

drawee of the cheque would be entitled to present the same.

On   such   presentation,   if   the   same    is   dishonored,   the

consequences contemplated under
Sec.138
and other provisions

of N.I.Act would flow. Under `the light of above said discussion

it is crystal clear that burden shifts on the accused to rebut the

legal presumption which exists in favour of the complainant by

proving that the cheque in       dispute was    not issued towards

discharge of amount claimed through cheque.
19. Hence complainant has placed sufficient oral and

documentary evidence to prove that        the accused issued the

cheque in discharge of legal liability.             Therefore,    the

presumption    under
Sec.139
of N.I.Act remain intact.            The

complainant has proved the complaint averments by placing oral

and documentary evidence.        On the other hand, the accused

failed to prove the defence by rebutting the above said

presumption.
20. The complainant has proved that the cheque in

question was issued by the accused towards discharge of legal

liability. The Honble Apex Court in several decisions starting

from Rangappa V/s       Mohan      held that presentation
u/s.118
and
139
of N.I.Act     comes into operation in favour of the

complainant that    the cheque in      question is issued towards

payment of any legal debt ,placing initial burden on the accused
14
C.C.No.33015 /2023
to rebut the same and establish the circumstances in which the

cheque in question reached the hands of the complainant. Only

after rebutting the above said presumption, burden, would shift

on the complainant to prove his case.
21. In the light of the above discussion and the material

placed on record, the court is of the opinion that the complainant

has proved existence of legal liability and also proved that the

accused    issued the cheque in question towards discharge of

legal liability. The complainant has proved the ingredients of
Sec.138
of N.I.Act. Therefore considering the entire facts and

circumstances of the case and the available evidence on record,

the court comes to the conclusion that the accused committed the

offence punishable under
Sec.138
of N.I.Act. The complainant is

entitled for compensation under
Sec.357
Cr.P.C. Therefore, the

complainant has proved point No.1 beyond all reasonable doubt.

Hence, Point No.1 & 2 are answered in the Affirmative
