                                 JUDGMENT
This is a suit filed by the plaintiff against the defendant for

recovery of Rs.3,25,000/- with interest @ 18% p.a. from the date

of vacating the schedule premises till its realization.
2. Nut shell of the plaintiff case are as under:

commercial property bearing No.172/24, Kilari Road, Avenue Road

Cross, Bangalore measuring East-West 16 feet, North-South 10

advocate by profession and intended to set up his office for which

in the year 2014 had approached the defendant and requested

premises on paying security deposit amount of Rs.3 lakhs and

Therefore, admitted facts need not be proved in view of
Sec.58
of

the Indian Evidence Act. Thus this court drawn its attention on
Sec.58
of the Indian Evidence Act which reads like this:
58 Facts admitted need not be proved. --No
     fact need to be proved in any proceeding which
     the parties thereto or their agents agree to
     admit at the hearing, or which, before the
     hearing, they agree to admit by any writing
     under their hands, or which by any rule of
     pleading in force at the time they are deemed
     to have admitted by their pleadings: Provided
     that the Court may, in its discretion, require
     the facts admitted to be proved otherwise than
     by such admissions.
14                   O.S.No.778/2021
The above provision is very much clear, no fact need to be proved

in any proceedings, which the parties thereto or their agents

agreed to admit at the hearing for which before the hearing they

agree to admit by any writing under their hands or which by any

rule of pleading in force at the time they were deemed to have

been admitted. Thus one thing is clear, the defendant has not

disputed neither receipt of Rs.3,25,000/- as security deposit nor

disputed the rent agreement which taken place in between the

taken place in between them nor receipt of Rs.3,25,000/- as

security deposit or disputed the monthly rent of Rs.4,200/-.

106. Burden of proving fact especially within

The above provision is very much clear when any fact is

Admittedly, the premises which involved in the rent agreement

has been let out by the defendant to the plaintiff for running

advocate office on monthly rent. Now the question arises if the

office is used exclusively for the purpose of advocate office

comes under commercial dispute, for which this court drawn its

attention on the judgment of Hon'ble High Court of Bombay

in W.P.No.6891/2010 in between
Rajendra G. Shah Vs
21                  O.S.No.778/2021
Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Company Ltd., In
the said case, the issue has been raised that the advocate

running the office in the residence and MSEDC has to charge as

non commercial purpose as domestic charges and suit has been

filed for declaration and injunction, it was came to be decreed

and appeal has been filed, it was came to be confirmed and

execution petition has been filed, wherein objection has been

raised which was upheld by the executing court and it was went

in appeal before the Hon'ble High Court of Bombay and the said

court held that the office running exclusively by the lawyers,

doctors, engineers, chartered accountants etc., comes under

commercial charges.
20. Thus in the instant case also though the plaintiff in the

instant case is not requesting to direct the defendant to let out

the premises nor the defendant has filed the suit for taking

possession from the plaintiff, however it is the specific case of the

plaintiff that he has paid Rs.3,25,000/- as security deposit to the

defendant in connection of the rent agreement, but the defendant

after vacating the premises which belongs to the defendant did

not return the said security deposit, in spite of repeated request

and demand, that is the reason why the plaintiff has filed the

instant suit against the defendant. Therefore, by virtue of the
22                 O.S.No.778/2021
judgment of the Hon'ble High Court of Bombay and the facts on

hand, the defendant has failed to prove that this court has no

jurisdiction to try this suit. On the other hand, the plaintiff has

proved   through   the   materials   that   this   court   having   the

jurisdiction to adjudicate the matter which is in dispute. Hence, I

am of the opinion that the issue No.1 and 2 answered as

Negative.

23                 O.S.No.778/2021
granting time for 6 months, thereby the defendant is liable to pay

the interest.
22. It is an admitted fact the plaintiff and the defendant

were entered into rent agreement dated 06.02.2014 and there is

no agreement in between them for payment of interest on

security deposit but the reasons best known to the defendant

soon after vacating the schedule premises by the plaintiff did not

repay the security deposit amount of Rs.3,25,000/- to the plaintiff,

as the Ex.P.3 is very much clear that the plaintif has addressed

the said letter to the defendant not only for vacating of sechedule

premises and taking of possession by the defendant and non

refunding   of   the   advance    secuirty   deposit   amount     of

Rs.3,25,000/-. So, if the recitals which appeared in Ex.P.3 is taken

into consideration, though the defendant has took the possession

from the plaintiff in the month of January 2019 but did not refund

the security deposit amount of Rs.3,25,000/- to the plaintiff. That

is the reason why the plaintiff has filed the instant suit against

the defendant, though the defendant has taken up the contention

that the plaintiff is due for arrears of rent for 48 months, but

nothing is placed on record to substantiate the same. However

has admitted that the plaintiff has paid advance security deposit

amount of Rs.3,25,000/-. So, one thing is clear from the
24                  O.S.No.778/2021
admission of the defendant and the materials on record, that the

plaintiff has vacated the sechedule premises of the defendant on

01.02.2019 and the defendant did not refund the security deposit

amount to the plaintiff. That is the reason why the plaintiff has

filed the instant suit against the defendant.
23. Now the question arises whether the plaintiff is entitle

the interest on the security deposit amount of Rs.3,25,000/-. Thus

this court drawn its attention on
Sec.3
of the Interest Act, 1978

which reads like this:
3. Power of court to allow interest.--
(1) In any proceedings for the recovery of any debt or damages or
     in any proceedings in which a claim for interest in respect of any
     debt or damages already paid is made, the court may, if it thinks
     fit, allow interest to the person entitled to the debt or damages or
     to the person making such claim, as the case may be, at a rate not
     exceeding the current rate of interest, for the whole or part of the
     following period, that is to say,--
(a) if the proceedings relate to a debt payable by virtue of a
     written instrument at a certain time, then, from the date when
     the debt is payable to the date of institution of the proceedings;
(b) if the proceedings do not relate to any such debt, then, from
     the date mentioned in this regard in a written notice given by the
     person entitled or the person making the claim to the person
     liable that interest will be claimed, to the date of institution of
     the proceedings: Provided that where the amount of the debt or
     damages has been repaid before the institution of the
     proceedings, interest shall not be allowed under this section for
     the period after such repayment.
The above provision is very much clear, in any proceeding for the

recovery of debt or damages or in any proceedings in which a
25                   O.S.No.778/2021
claim for interest is made the court may if it thinks fit allow the

interest. In the instant case, though the plaintiff has been vacated

the schedule premises of the defendant on 01.02.2019, but the

defendant did not refund the security deposit amount of

Rs.3,25,000/- without any reasons, though has taken up the

arrears of rent which not proved by the defendant, thereby the

defendant is liable to pay the interest @ 18% p.a. on security

deposit amount of Rs.3,25,000/- from the date of vacating of the

schedule premises till its realization it will meet the ends of

justice. Hence, I am of the opinion that the issue No.3 answered

as affirmative.

    realization.
